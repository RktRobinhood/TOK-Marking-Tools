Tutorial

How to use the planner

Build the reasoning before the prose

An outline here is not a list of paragraphs. It is a map of your argument: each Block holds one move in your reasoning, and the way Blocks sit under one another shows what supports what.

Nothing in this planner is graded, and every Block you start with can be rewritten or deleted.

1. Add a Block

The Toolbox on the left shows the kinds of reasoning move that make sense where you are working. There are two ways to bring one in.

  • Drag the card onto the outline. While you drag, orange lines appear between the existing Blocks — drop on the line where the new Block belongs.
  • Or press the card's + button. That adds the Block to the end of the Section you are currently working in, which is the one drawn with a coloured frame.

2. Show what supports what

Indenting a Block means "this exists to support the Block above it". This is the single most important thing the planner records, because it is what an examiner reads as argument rather than as a list.

  • Use a Block's indent and outdent controls to change what it sits under.
  • A Block can go two levels deep at most. That limit is deliberate — if you need a third level, the reasoning usually wants splitting into two Blocks instead.
  • Moving a Block carries everything nested under it, so a supported claim never gets separated from its support.

3. Reorder, duplicate, remove

Order is an argument too. If your evaluation arrives before the reasoning it evaluates, the outline will show you.

  • Duplicate is useful when two Blocks share a shape — copy the Block, then rewrite the copy.
  • Every Block can be deleted, including the ones your starting layout gave you. An empty Section is a valid outline.
  • Anything you undo by accident comes back with Undo in the top bar.

4. Write inside a Block

Click into a Block to open it. Predefined Blocks show a grey Helper Prompt — a question to answer, not text to keep.

  • The Helper Prompt disappears as soon as you write your own content — it is scaffolding, and it never reaches your teacher or the AI.
  • Rename the Block title to what your reasoning actually claims. "Why the method matters" tells you far more later than "Analysis" does.
  • Bold, italic and bullet lists are available while a Block is open. Collapse a finished Block to get the whole shape back on screen.

5. Choose, explore, and order your AOKs

Choose from the five official Areas of Knowledge. Introduction stays first and Conclusion stays last, while your two selected AOK Sections can trade places as complete units.

  • Use Explore another AOK if you are not ready to settle on a pair. The extra AOK keeps its own Blocks in a separate exploration workspace.
  • The AOK combinations tray lets you try a different AOK 1 or AOK 2 without deleting the AOK you replace.
  • Only the two marked Selected for essay appear in the Teacher PDF or AI feedback prompt. Every candidate remains in the Working Copy.

Swap the selected pair if the essay feels back-to-front. The AOK that sets up the problem usually wants to come first.

6. Keep your work

This planner has no account and no server. Read this step carefully — it is the one that protects your work.

  • Press Save Working Copy (.html) to download your outline. The file contains both your work and the planner itself.
  • To carry on later, open that downloaded file — it reopens as a working planner with everything where you left it, with no internet connection needed.
  • Save a fresh copy at the end of every session. Each download is a new file, so keeping several is a free version history.

7. Print it for your teacher

Export PDF / Print opens your browser's print dialog; choose "Save as PDF" to get a file you can hand in.

  • Collapsed Blocks are opened automatically in the printout, so your teacher always sees the whole outline.
  • Buttons, handles and Helper Prompts are stripped out. Only your argument prints.
  • Your name, title and AOKs need to be filled in before printing — the planner will tell you if any are missing.

8. Ask an AI for feedback

Ask AI for Feedback asks you what kind of help you want, then downloads a file containing your whole outline plus instructions for the AI. Paste the entire file into any AI chat.

  • All three refuse to write your essay. They diagnose, question and flag — they will not give you arguments, examples or sentences to copy. That is deliberate: the thinking has to be yours.
  • The AI's reply does not come back into the planner. You decide what is worth acting on and change the outline yourself.
  • The AI sees the Block IDs, so it can point at exactly which Block it means.

At a glance

Three gestures are enough to reshape an outline.

1. Add deliberately

Drag a Toolbox card to a line, or use its + button to append it.

2. Show support

Move, indent, or outdent Blocks. A parent carries its nested group.

3. Compare and export

Swap AOK order, save a Working Copy, then print it or ask AI for feedback.
